Every building gets a use: classifier, then census

Draft

A gradient-boosting classifier gives all 6.4M buildings use probabilities from their shape, height and surroundings. Model A learns from tags alone, model B adds the LLM labels of the deep dives. Held out, B ranks uses better (AUC 0.78 by district on tags, 0.76 by sub-district on LLM labels) and, unlike A, learns ancillary structures (AUC 0.82). The census then decides how many: workshops, services, shops and 3.31M households are placed on the most likely buildings, rarest first, so every district reproduces its census exactly. One home per building the classifier calls residential would give 1.7× too many households.

Grid-connection model

Draft Trained on restricted DHS data; no cluster locations shown

A logistic regression predicts whether a household is connected, from open neighbourhood features averaged over the DHS displacement radius (2 km urban, 5 km rural): population and building density, wealth, distance to the predicted MV line and roads, building size, settlement class, cropland. Held out by district it reaches AUC 0.75 (urban/rural rate alone: 0.68; a perfect neighbourhood model: 0.89) and, by district, tracks EICV7 2024 grid lighting closely (r = 0.93). It gives a neighbourhood's probability: two buildings side by side get the same value.

Constraints, years and uncertainty

Draft

Since 2026-10-15 every aggregate we hold is a constraint: census households per district (urban and rural), Establishment Census counts, MINEDUC schools, a national count of places of worship, DHS 2025 wealth by district, EICV7 2024 grid shares and REG consumption. They can no longer validate the model; instead we report how much the answer moves. Ten allocations with perturbed scores keep sub-district totals within a few percent, while which building holds a shop or church changes often (36% agreement). With a quarter of rural manufacturing as mills (2,271 nationally), Nasho gets 22 mills; without spacing caps it would get 26, with up to 39 mills in one block elsewhere. Back-cast: building presence 2016–2023 places growth, pinned to census household growth (3.1%/yr).

Against REG sales, customers and health-facility counts

Independent check

From the NISR Statistical Yearbook 2024 (REG tables), RURA's Q2 2024 electricity statistics and the EICV3–5 thematic reports. Households' expected demand is 178 / 193 / 208 GWh in 2021 / 2022 / 2023 against REG residential sales of 154 / 179 / 211 GWh, and connected households per REG branch correlate at r = 0.93 across the 27 branches outside Kigali. Two gaps stand out: health facilities (55 GWh modelled vs 22.7 sold; SEforALL's 2023 site surveys point to lower health-centre use) and businesses (78 GWh modelled vs 257 sold to non-residential and small-industry customers; large hotels are missing).

Known limitations

  1. Google 2.5D heights are compressed: single-storey buildings read ~4–5 m, so floors above 2 are unreliable.
  2. First-seen year has artefacts (jumps in 2019 and 2023); it is experimental.
  3. Use labels come from open vector data only and agree with human labels ~40% of the time; the human labels are themselves uncertain.
  4. Microsoft footprints omit structures under ~15 m².
  5. Cooling demand is marginal in most of Rwanda; the category is a relative proxy.
  6. Labels cover the three deep dives (Muhoza partly) and a national sample.
  7. Several business and institution profiles rest on assumptions (signed off); health facilities total 55 GWh/yr against REG's 22.7 GWh: health centres are scaled to MTF spending (far above SEforALL's measured 5–11 kWh/day) and 127 tagged hospitals are twice MoH's 63 (pending).
  8. Businesses are under-counted: modelled establishments use about 30% of REG's non-residential and small-industry sales, and large hotels are missing.
  9. Household energy is scaled to REG means; outside Kigali this puts rural households at 4.4 kWh/month, below QSEL's rural survey (8.1): the two sources disagree.
  10. Rural households in the two wealth halves spend almost the same (~10 kWh/month). The upper share follows DHS 2025 per district (36% rural, 55% urban nationally).
