## Source layout (resolved)
- HDX zip `urls/RWA_<year>.txt`: 260 tiles/year, the same tile ids every year, at
  `https://storage.googleapis.com/open-buildings-temporal-data/v1/geotiffs/<s2token>_<year>_06_30/tile_<id>.tif`.
- Each tile is 12.5 km x 12.5 km, 0.5 m pixels, 3 float32 bands (1 fractional_count, 2 height, 3 presence),
  nodata -99, DEFLATE, internal overviews 2x/4x/7x/13x/... The full set is ~41 GB/year (326 GB for 2016-2023).
- **Tiles use their local UTM zone: 161 are in 35S and 99 in 36S (east of 30 deg E).** My first national run
  missed this, because a single VRT silently dropped the 36S tiles. The code now projects per tile and builds one
  VRT per zone, and there is a test with a 36S tile. 1.64 M buildings fall in 36S tiles.

## Storage strategy (`config/sources.yaml: google_25d_temporal.mode`)
`remote` (used): GDAL range reads over /vsicurl/, asking for a coarser grid so GDAL reads the internal overview
instead of the 0.5 m data. About 3 GB is transferred, and nothing goes to data/raw except the URL index.
`mode: full` downloads whole tiles through `manifest.fetch` (immutable raw, sha256) and derives the same
rasters; `height_res_m` / `presence_res_m` are switches too.

**Resolution check on the fixture** (2,244 buildings, Nyamirambo; read against native 0.5 m):
| grid | what | agreement with 0.5 m |
|---|---|---|
| height 2 m | footprint median | bias -0.08 m, MAE 0.22 m, p95 abs err 0.62 m; est_floors identical for 90.6% |
| presence 2 m | 2023 centroid >= 0.5 | 92.4% |
| presence **4 m (used)** | 2023 centroid >= 0.5 | 86.4% (0.3: 91.2%, 0.7: 87.7%) |
| presence 8 m | 2023 centroid >= 0.5 | 68.6% (0.3: 82.1%, 0.7: 69.5%) |

I used 4 m, not the suggested 8-16 m. 8 m loses too much: in 2023 it misclassifies about 1 in 3 centroids at the
0.5 threshold, and first_seen nulls rise from 39% to 50%. 4 m equals the product's effective resolution and costs
~0.2 GB/year. The loss is not specific to small buildings: agreement at 8 m was 68.6% both for footprints under
30 m2 and for larger ones. First-seen year at 4 m matches the 2 m result for 73% of buildings (|diff| <= 1 year
mostly), so treat single-year attribution as approximate.

## Definitions
- `height_m`: coverage-weighted **median** of 2023 height pixels (2 m) under the footprint. Misregistration
  between footprints and the 4 m product puts edge pixels on ground (0 m) or on a taller neighbour; the median
  ignores both while most of the footprint is on its own roof (planted-raster test: mean 4.8 m vs median 6.0 m).
  exactextract's built-in `median` interpolates between *distinct* values (90 px @ 6 m + 10 px @ 9 m -> 7.5 m), so
  the median is computed explicitly. Fallback to the centroid pixel when the footprint covers < 1 pixel (1,960
  buildings). A value < 1 m (no structure seen) is stored as null. `height_source` =
  `google_25d_temporal:2023:footprint_median_2m` (or `centroid_2m`).
- `first_seen_year`: earliest year with centroid presence >= 0.5 **in that year and every later year** (flicker
  can neither create nor delay a building). I sample the centroid, not the footprint max, because a max over
  4 m pixels picks up neighbouring roofs in dense areas and would date buildings too early. 2016 = "2016 or earlier".
  Null = not stably present through 2023 (never detected, demolished, or presence < threshold in 2023).
- `est_floors` = max(1, round_half_up(height_m / 3.0)), capped at 30; `gfa_m2` = area_m2 x est_floors.

## Height validation vs OSM (`reports/figures/A1_height_vs_osm.png`)
12,088 OSM building polygons carry `building:levels` or `height`. 10,405 matched by footprint centroid inside the
OSM polygon (largest footprint per polygon), and 9,846 of those have a 2.5D height.
- est_floors vs `building:levels` (n = 9,846; 97% are 1-level): **bias +0.52 floors, MAE 0.69, exact 42.6%,
  within +/-1 93.1%**, r = 0.45.
- height_m vs levels x 3 m: bias +1.47 m, MAE 2.16 m. Median 2.5D height by OSM levels:
  1 -> 4.7 m, 2 -> 6.0, 3 -> 6.2, 4 -> 7.2, 5 -> 8.4, 6+ -> 12.0. **The 2.5D height is compressed**: single-storey
  roofs read ~4.7 m (pitched roofs, offset) and tall buildings are strongly underestimated.
- height_m vs OSM `height` tag: only n = 34, bias -5.8 m, MAE 8.0 m (dominated by a few 20-50 m towers read as 2-7 m).
- Storey-height sensitivity (floors vs OSM levels): 3.0 m exact 42.6% / bias +0.52; 3.5 m 61.9% / +0.27;
  4.0 m 77.1% / +0.08; 4.5 m 85.2% / -0.03; 5.0 m 88.5% / -0.09. For OSM levels >= 2 (n = 787), exact agreement
  *falls* as the storey height grows (29.7% at 3 m, 17.7% at 4.5 m).

## Decisions for the human
1. **Storey height.** Kept 3.0 m as specified, but OSM evidence says it turns about half of single-storey
   buildings into 2 floors, which doubles their GFA. 4.0-4.5 m removes the bias overall but under-counts real
   multi-storey buildings. One option: a calibrated mapping height -> floors (e.g. 1 floor below 5.5 m), fitted on
   OSM levels with a held-out split. Note that OSM is then used for calibration, not validation.
2. **Presence threshold** for first_seen_year: 0.5 leaves 67% null; 0.3 leaves 48% null. Choose based on whether
   you need coverage or precision for growth.
3. **Full download?** `mode: full` (41 GB/year) only matters if 0.5 m presence is needed; at 2 m the height loss
   is negligible.

## Proposed contract changes (not made; schemas.py is the lead's)

## Update 2026-10-09 (lead)
- Floors now come from fitted height cut-points (`config/params.yaml: floors_method: thresholds`): 1 floor below
  6.0 m, 2 floors below 8.5 m, then +1 per 3 m. Fitted on even OSM ids, tested on odd ids (n = 4,927). Test exact
  match: 83% for 1 storey, 33% for 2, 10% for 3+ (MAE 0.20 / 0.73 / 2.34 floors), against a single 3.4 m storey
  height at MAE 0.41 / 0.58 / 2.12. The single-storey-height option stays available (`floors_method: storey`).
- First-seen threshold stays at 0.5 (33% of buildings get a year). The `presence_2016`–`presence_2023` columns allow
  re-tuning: 0.4 gives 43%, 0.3 gives 52%. Change `presence_threshold` and run `pixi run derive` (~2 min, no download).
